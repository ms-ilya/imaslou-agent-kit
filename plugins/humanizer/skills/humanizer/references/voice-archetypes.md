# Voice archetypes

Reference points, not quotas or permission to invent a persona. Source voice, explicit user preferences, language and content integrity take precedence. English contractions have no automatic Russian equivalent. Preserve uncertainty wherever the source expresses it, including marketing and release notes.

## Speaker versus expression

Keep speaker identity, stance, degree of certainty and communicative intent fixed. Rhythm, sentence frames and generic framing are expression choices, not identity. Freeze reader, genre, register and purpose in the run brief before drafting; carry it through every revision. A source with generic presentation does not require reproducing those habits. Recompose within this brief while preserving deliberate rhetoric and meaningful emphasis. No persona lottery, mandatory informality or voice amplification.

## Choose the relevant voice

Identify who will publish or speak the text and who will read it. Follow the current request and explicit preferences first, then the relevant publisher's supplied guide/profile, then compatible habits supported by supplied samples, then the source and genre. A writer's casual personal sample does not govern an organization's formal notice merely because that writer drafted it. Within any source, a stated preference outranks a habit inferred from examples. Content integrity, protected text and scope still govern every choice.

Use a profile in its supplied format; no template conversion or setup interview is required. Read sources the user provides or points to, and applicable style instructions already in context. Do not search for profile files by convention, create or save a profile, or change persistent preferences without a request. If a requested source cannot be read, disclose that limit and do not claim to have matched it. With no source, continue in the source/genre voice; no invented house style or mandatory sample request.

## When the user supplies writing samples or a guide

Treat samples as evidence of expression, separate from the source being rewritten. Use relevant samples when supplied; without them, infer voice from the source and genre and continue without requesting samples by default.

1. **Check relevance.** Compare language, audience, genre and purpose. A casual message can suggest direct phrasing for a status note, but does not justify slang in formal instructions. Do not translate a sample's English devices into Russian rules. With short or conflicting samples, use only justified, compatible features; ask only when an unresolved authoritative conflict would materially change the edit.
2. **Describe the evidence internally.** Separate explicit preferences from observed habits and weak/unknown evidence. Note register, typical syntax, paragraph openings, transitions, preferred phrasing, punctuation, degree of directness and use of explanation. Support a useful observation with a short sample span or guide section. Distinguish recurring choices from one topic's vocabulary, quoted speech, a typo or an isolated flourish. Do not infer a personality, biography or precise numerical rates from a small sample.
3. **Apply compatible features to the source's message.** A sample may support shorter setup, connected clauses or an existing conversational tone. It supplies no facts, identity, anecdotes, opinions, confidence or permission to change the speaker. Its “I” or “we” does not authorize first-person testimony in a third-person source. Do not import the sample's narrative or reproduce errors as a style device.
4. **Review the actual draft.** Compare expression with the selected evidence and content with the complete original. Check for sample facts leaking into the rewrite, forced informality, repeated sample-like openings, catchphrases pasted into unrelated passages and lost qualifications. Source integrity and explicit scope/style instructions override sampled habits; language grammar and technical precision still apply. Keep a neutral source neutral unless the requested style change supports a compatible adjustment. A broad genre resemblance does not establish distinctive author matching; report only the features actually supported.

For example, a sample saying “First, let's check the numbers. I tried this last week” can support direct sequencing. Given “The team must compare the logs before deciding whether to expand the trial,” a compatible rewrite is “Before deciding whether to expand the trial, the team needs to compare the logs.” It does not become “I tried this last week” or “Let's expand the trial.” The sample's experience and the decision remain separate.

If a formal organizational guide accompanies an author's casual sample, a release note such as “The release adds CSV export; approval remains required” can become “The release adds CSV export. Approval is still required.” It does not acquire the personal sample's “I shipped it” or lose the approval requirement. A personal email from that author may instead use the casual evidence; the intended publisher and audience decide relevance.

## Deliberate choices in this task

When the user says to keep wording or a stylistic choice, carry that exception through drafting, self-review and the judge's request context. Do not repeatedly flag the same retained choice as an unresolved style defect. For example, an explicitly retained three-part refrain can stay while a separate unclear sentence is repaired. This is a task-specific exception, not a permanent preference or authority to alter other protected content. New factual, grammatical or scope errors still require review; preserving an intentional construction does not excuse introducing one.

## Genre calibration

| Text type | Voice considerations | Rhythm and fragments |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Blog / essay / opinion | Preserve the author's existing position and person | Follow argument and established style; no forced asides |
| Technical documentation | Precise terms, actors, conditions and ordered instructions | Complete sentences or concise list items as appropriate |
| Email / message | Match relationship, formality and intended action | Short paragraphs/fragments where natural; no required contractions |
| Marketing / landing page | Preserve actual benefits, evidence and limits | Concise wording; no invented confidence or results |
| Academic / formal | Keep attribution, calibrated hedges and terminology | Complex sentences may be necessary; preserve logical links |
| Social media / threads | Match established conversational voice | Abbreviations/fragments only when fitting the source and audience |
| Press release / news | Keep reported claims attributed and time-bound | Clear progression and useful summaries |
| Changelog / release notes | Preserve versions, scope, limitations and change semantics | Parallel bullets often help scanning |
| Creative / narrative | Preserve intentional voice, irony, repetition and perspective | Rhythm is part of the craft; avoid automatic normalization |
| Legal / compliance | Keep obligations, permissions, exceptions and defined terms exact | Clarity cannot justify weakening qualifications or dependencies |
| Conversational / chat | Preserve speaker intent and register | Existing informal syntax may be appropriate; no added persona |
| Mixed / unknown | Identify local voices; default to source-neutral wording when uncertain | Preserve deliberate shifts and useful navigation |

Neutral stance permits source-compatible imagery, emphasis and idiom; it does not require flat expression. Pronouns in quotations, code or a chatbot wrapper do not authorize first-person claims in the body. If changing a voice would change meaning or the user's intent, keep it.
